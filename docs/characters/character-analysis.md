# Character Analysis

> Status: Portfolio summary
> Source note: [character-analysis.ko.md](character-analysis.ko.md)

This document summarizes the character-side balance philosophy for ReadTheRoom.

## Core Balance Idea

Characters are not defined only by which stats are high or low. They are defined by how their stats collapse under pressure.

Each character starts with a different kind of vulnerability:

- Some characters begin socially strong but physically fragile.
- Some characters begin inexperienced but grow quickly.
- Some characters have money or confidence but lose stability in specific situations.

The design goal is delayed consequence. Starting stats are resources with an expiration date, not permanent advantages.

## Tier 1 Character Direction

Tier 1 characters are intended to be immediately playable and easy to understand, while still teaching different survival patterns.

### Jina

Jina is a relationship-centered character with high social awareness, but her stamina is fragile.

Design identity:

- Strong relationship and social reading ability.
- Pregnancy and settlement stress create a fragile mental and physical curve.
- High early confidence can become dangerous if the player ignores stamina.

Primary lesson:

```text
Social strength cannot replace pacing and recovery.
```

### Ken

Ken is a growth-oriented international student.

Design identity:

- Low starting English and moderate insight create early helplessness.
- Youth and stamina allow him to endure embarrassment and rejection.
- Growth choices are intended to reward active learning.

Current design note:

- Ken's planned passive effect is a bonus to English and insight gains from Growth choices.
- This is documented as a design direction, not necessarily guaranteed as current runtime behavior.

Primary lesson:

```text
Failure can become progress if the player chooses to learn from it.
```

### Amy

Amy is a practical working-holiday character.

Design identity:

- Work experience and emotional warmth make her approachable.
- Service-industry situations can stress mental stamina and social judgment.
- Her route is suited for realistic everyday-life pressure.

Primary lesson:

```text
Practical survival depends on boundaries, money awareness, and emotional control.
```

## Design Use

This character analysis is useful for:

- Choosing starting stats.
- Designing character-specific event pressure.
- Keeping scenario consequences consistent with character identity.
- Avoiding characters that feel different only through cosmetic differences.

## Portfolio Notes

The character system is designed around readable tradeoffs. A player should understand why a character feels different within the first few choices, and the story should gradually pressure the exact weaknesses that make that character interesting.
